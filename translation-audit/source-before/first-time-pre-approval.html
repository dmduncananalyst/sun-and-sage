California and San Fernando Valley context

Financing a San Fernando Valley home should be evaluated with the actual purchase price and property costs, not just a headline loan amount. Dominique can help with the real estate side while a licensed lender explains qualification, rates, cash to close, and loan program rules.

Understanding a Preliminary Lending Decision

A preapproval or prequalification letter can help establish a starting point for a search, but it does not complete the financing process. Ask what income, funds, debts, and credit information were reviewed, how current that review is, and which conditions remain. Lenders do not always use the two terms in exactly the same way.

A selected property still needs to meet applicable appraisal, title, insurance, and program requirements. Keep the lender informed about changes to income, employment, debts, or available purchase funds. The useful purpose of an early review is to understand a realistic starting position and unresolved issues, rather than to treat the letter as unconditional final approval.

Understanding Written Loan Terms

Compare financing using the written terms for your application and property. The loan amount, repayment period, rate structure, mortgage insurance, lender charges, and cash required at closing work together. A change in one part of the offer can affect the others, so a quoted monthly principal and interest payment is only part of the comparison.

Ask which conditions remain before approval and what changes require a new review. New debt, a change in employment or documented income, or a property issue can affect financing before closing. Use the lender’s current disclosures and explanations rather than relying on a general program description as a promise of eligibility or final terms.
