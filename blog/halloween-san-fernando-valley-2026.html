Halloween Activities in and Around the San Fernando Valley

September 24, 2026

Updated October 14, 2026

Halloween around the San Fernando Valley can mean a glowing pumpkin trail, a movie under the stars, or an evening at a community event. The most enjoyable choice depends on the kind of experience your group wants, from a relaxed seasonal outing to a deliberately frightening attraction.

October also gives you more than one opportunity to celebrate. A planned evening earlier in the month can leave Halloween night free for your own neighborhood, without trying to combine a major attraction, dinner, and trick or treating into one rushed trip.

Pumpkin displays beyond the neighborhood porch

Night of the Jack at King Gillette Ranch in Calabasas offers a walking experience built around illuminated pumpkin displays. Its ranch setting makes it a different outing from visiting a decorated residential street, with the landscape and changing light becoming part of the evening.

Think about the walk as well as the photographs. Comfortable shoes, your group’s preferred pace, and the time of entry all influence the experience. Before booking, use the organizer’s schedule to confirm the operating date, arrival arrangements, accessibility information, and any rules about what visitors may bring.

Calabasas is a nearby destination for a Valley outing, so allow for the journey as part of the plan. A ticketed visit works best when there is enough time to arrive without turning the first part of the evening into a race against the entry window.

A Halloween movie night in Mission Hills

Valleyween Cinema has published 2026 experiences featuring Hotel Transylvania and Hocus Pocus at Andres Pico Adobe Park in Mission Hills. A seasonal movie event gives a group a shared activity to gather around, especially when everyone wants Halloween atmosphere without choosing a haunted maze.

Review the information for the particular performance rather than assuming that every night has the same arrangements. The event’s current listing should explain the date, arrival time, seating, included activities, and what guests need to bring. Those details determine whether the outing suits the people coming with you.

A movie night can also be a useful option for a mixed group. Some visitors may want the costumes and decorations more than a scare, while others may be happy simply spending an evening together outside.

Community events close to home

School and neighborhood calendars can offer another kind of celebration. Valley Charter Elementary’s published school calendar lists a Trunk or Treat on October 24, 2026. Events organized around a school or community can provide a more contained setting than traveling between several attractions.

Check who may attend and whether registration is needed before treating a school event as open admission. A calendar entry gives a starting point, while the organizer’s current details establish access, timing, and participation arrangements.

Community events can be particularly appealing when the goal is a manageable evening near home. They give you a chance to enjoy the season without making a large attraction the center of the plan.

Choose the intensity that fits your group

A pumpkin display, a costumed community gathering, and a haunted attraction are different experiences. Darkness, loud sound, enclosed spaces, uneven surfaces, and performers can affect how comfortable a visitor feels. Age recommendations are worth reviewing alongside the description of the event itself.

There is no need to make the biggest event the default choice. One well chosen outing can be more enjoyable than filling every weekend, especially when your household already has school activities, work, and other autumn plans.
