Trick or Treating in the San Fernando Valley

Posted October 4, 2026

There is more than one way to trick or treat in the Valley

Trick or treating in the San Fernando Valley can mean walking a residential neighborhood on Halloween night, going to a school or community trunk or treat, or choosing an organized event with candy stations. The best option depends on the age of the kids, how much walking your group wants to do, and whether you prefer a neighborhood experience or a planned event with a set start and end time.

For traditional neighborhood trick or treating

If your family wants the classic door to door experience, choose a residential area with sidewalks, good lighting, and homes that are clearly participating. Go before it gets too late, keep younger children with an adult, use reflective or lighted accessories when costumes are dark, and respect homes that do not have decorations or porch lights on. Parking a little away from the busiest streets can also make the evening easier.

For a more organized option

Trunk or treat events can be a good alternative for younger children or families that want candy in one contained location. Valley Charter Elementary lists its 2026 Trunk or Treat for October 24, with trunk or treating, a costume parade, crafts, face painting, food, popcorn, and hot cocoa. Events like this can make the night feel more predictable because families know where the activities are and when they end.

Valleyween adds trick or treating to movie night

Valleyween Cinema at Andres Pico Adobe Park in Mission Hills is another option for families who want more than just candy. The October 30 and October 31, 2026 events list trick or treating at themed stations along with character meet and greets, music, popcorn, drinks, and an outdoor movie. This can work well if your group wants a Halloween activity first and trick or treating as part of the evening rather than the entire plan.

Check community calendars close to home

Halloween event calendars for the San Fernando Valley list multiple trick or treat and trunk or treat events throughout late October. Before choosing a neighborhood or driving to an event, check the organizer's current page for the time, parking instructions, age recommendations, registration requirements, and any changes caused by weather or capacity.

A few simple things make the night easier

Comfortable shoes matter more than perfect costume shoes, especially if kids will be walking for an hour or more. Bring water, carry a flashlight or phone light, make sure masks do not block vision, and inspect candy before younger children eat it. If your group includes different ages, decide in advance whether everyone will stay together or whether older kids have a specific meeting point and check in time.

Make the night about the neighborhood too

One of the fun parts of Halloween in the Valley is seeing how different communities celebrate. Some streets go all in with lights and decorations, while others are quieter and more relaxed. It is a small reminder that choosing a neighborhood is about more than the house, it is also about the kind of everyday community experience you want around you.
