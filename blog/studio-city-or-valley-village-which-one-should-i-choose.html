Studio City or Valley Village, Which One Should I Choose?

October 3, 2026

Start with the biggest difference, price

If you are choosing between Studio City and Valley Village, budget may narrow the decision before anything else does. Dominique’s city pages, with market data current as of August 2026, place Studio City’s median home value at approximately $1.8 million to $1.9 million, while Valley Village is approximately $1.1 million to $1.14 million.

That does not mean every Studio City home costs more than every Valley Village home. Condos, townhomes, lot size, condition, street, and location within the neighborhood can change the numbers quickly. It does mean buyers may be comparing two very different price points when they start with detached homes.

Studio City feels more connected to Ventura Boulevard

Studio City has a strong commercial spine along Ventura Boulevard. Restaurants, grocery stores, fitness studios, cafes, and everyday services are concentrated there, so buyers who like being close to dining and activity may naturally spend more time around that corridor.

The neighborhood also gives you quick access toward Hollywood, Burbank, Universal City, and other parts of the Valley. The Universal City and Studio City Metro B Line station adds another transportation option for people who do not want every trip to depend on a car.

Valley Village has a quieter residential feel

Valley Village is close to Studio City and North Hollywood, but much of the neighborhood feels more residential. You can still reach restaurants, grocery stores, cafes, and entertainment nearby, while living on streets that may feel more removed from the busiest commercial areas.

That balance can appeal to buyers who want access to the same general part of the Valley without needing to live directly beside its most active corridors.

The homes can feel different too

Studio City includes hillside homes, larger single family properties, condos, townhomes, and homes near Ventura Boulevard. Some hillside locations bring views and privacy, but they can also bring additional questions about slope, wildfire exposure, drainage, and insurance.

Valley Village is flatter overall and includes a mix of detached homes, condos, townhomes, and smaller multifamily buildings. Buyers who prefer a flatter neighborhood and simpler day to day access may find that important.

Think about how you actually spend your week

If your ideal weekend means walking to a restaurant, being near Ventura Boulevard, heading toward Hollywood, or spending time around Studio City’s busier commercial areas, Studio City may fit the way you already live.

If you want a quieter home base, still want quick access to Studio City and North Hollywood, and would rather put more of your budget toward the home itself, Valley Village may deserve a closer look.

Schools, transportation, and monthly costs still matter

Both neighborhoods are served by LAUSD, but school assignment is property specific, so buyers should verify the assigned schools for the exact address they are considering.

Condo and townhome buyers should also compare HOA dues, parking, insurance, reserves, and the full monthly payment instead of comparing purchase price alone. A lower purchase price does not always mean a lower monthly cost.

So, which one should you choose?

Choose based on the life you want to have after you get the keys. Studio City may make more sense if you want more activity nearby, direct access to Ventura Boulevard, and are comfortable with the higher price point. Valley Village may make more sense if you want a quieter residential setting, a lower overall entry point, and easy access to nearby Studio City and North Hollywood.

The best comparison is not neighborhood versus neighborhood in the abstract. It is the actual Studio City home you could buy with your budget versus the actual Valley Village home you could buy with that same budget.
