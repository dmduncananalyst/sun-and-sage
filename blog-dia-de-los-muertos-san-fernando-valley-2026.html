Celebrating Día de los Muertos in the San Fernando Valley

October 12, 2026

Día de los Muertos is a time to remember loved ones through flowers, photographs, food and shared stories. In the San Fernando Valley, San Fernando Mission Cemetery has announced its 2026 observance for Saturday, November 7, with Mass at noon and a cultural celebration beginning at 1:30 p.m.

A celebration of memory and love

Día de los Muertos, traditionally observed around November 1 and 2, brings remembrance into everyday life. Its Mexican traditions combine Indigenous heritage and Catholic observances, with practices that vary between families and communities.

An ofrenda is a personal tribute. Photographs recall familiar faces, marigolds bring color, and favorite foods or treasured objects connect the altar to the person being remembered. Pan de muerto and papel picado are familiar parts of many celebrations, but every family’s observance has its own meaning.

For children, an altar can become a way to hear stories about relatives they knew and those they never had the chance to meet. For adults, it offers a place to pause, remember and share something that might otherwise go unsaid.

A confirmed 2026 celebration in Mission Hills

San Fernando Mission Cemetery, at 11160 Stranwood Avenue in Mission Hills, will hold its Día de los Muertos observance on Saturday, November 7, 2026. The organizer’s published schedule lists Mass and the blessing of the altars at noon, followed by music, children’s activities and folkloric dancers at 1:30 p.m. The event ends at 6 p.m.

The cemetery setting makes remembrance central to the occasion. Visitors can take time to appreciate the altars while respecting the families who created them and the people resting there.

Check Catholic Cemeteries & Mortuaries for the latest event information before attending. If you hope to display an altar, check registration directly with the organizer rather than assuming space is available. The published page currently marks San Fernando altar registration as closed.

Finding celebrations close to home

The City of San Fernando and other Valley communities share seasonal cultural programming through their own calendars and announcements. Look for current organizer information before choosing a date, since a previous year’s schedule may still appear in an event roundup.

A public celebration can be a thoughtful introduction to the tradition. Go with curiosity, listen to the stories being shared, and ask before photographing a family’s altar or participating in a private observance.

The meaning of home

A home holds more than furniture and belongings. It holds the meals people shared, the conversations that ran late, and the small routines that become family memories.

An ofrenda brings those memories into view. A photograph on a shelf or a familiar recipe on the table can remind us how much of home is shaped by the people we love.
