Los Angeles Short-Term Rental Rules in 2026: What Valley Homeowners Must Know Before Hosting

October 9, 2026

In the City of Los Angeles, home-sharing generally requires your primary residence, city registration, and a registration number on advertisements. Standard registration allows up to 120 days per calendar year; extended home-sharing needs separate approval. Unincorporated Los Angeles County has its own program, so confirm your property’s jurisdiction before planning a rental.

City of Los Angeles: the home-sharing basics

The Home-Sharing Ordinance governs eligible short-term rentals within city boundaries. Ownership alone does not make a second home eligible.

  • Primary residence: you must reside in the home for more than six months of the year.
  • Annual limit: standard home-sharing is capped at 120 days in a calendar year. An extended registration requires additional eligibility, approval, and fees.
  • Registration: register and renew through City Planning, and display the required registration number in advertisements. Check the current portal for fees rather than relying on an old guide.
  • Tax: the city’s Transient Occupancy Tax is 14% for qualifying stays of 30 days or less. Confirm which obligations your platform handles and which remain yours.
  • Records: retain required booking records for at least three years.

The city also requires a Police Commission permit for short-term rental operations under rules effective July 1, 2024. Review the current permit instructions and the city’s registration requirements together.

What can disqualify a property

Rent-stabilized units covered by the city’s RSO and units subject to qualifying affordability restrictions are not eligible for home-sharing. A renter needs the required notarized landlord authorization. Unresolved violations can also affect registration.

ADUs require special care. Eligibility depends on permit history, occupancy, and applicable restrictions; a qualifying ADU must itself satisfy the city’s primary-residence conditions. Some state-authorized ADUs also carry minimum rental-term restrictions. Do not assume that a backyard unit can be rented nightly simply because the main house is your home.

Unincorporated LA County has a separate program

City of Los Angeles rules do not govern every Los Angeles County address. Incorporated cities such as Burbank and San Fernando have their own requirements, and unincorporated county areas use a separate county program.

The county ordinance provides for registration through the Treasurer and Tax Collector and lists a $914 annual registration fee; confirm the current fee before applying. Its rules include a primary-residence requirement, a registration affidavit, and a 90-night annual limit for unhosted rentals. ADUs are prohibited under that county program. Do not apply the city’s 120-day standard to an unincorporated county property.

Use the city’s ZIMAS address lookup or county jurisdiction maps to determine the governing authority. A mailing address alone is not enough to establish which rules apply.

The midterm alternative still has rules

A rental longer than 30 days may fall outside the city’s home-sharing program, making furnished midterm stays an option worth evaluating for relocating households or traveling professionals. That does not make the rental exempt from other housing requirements.

Tenant protections, rent stabilization, ADU permit conditions, HOA restrictions, lease terms, and insurance can still apply. A city tax exemption for a longer stay also has documentation requirements, including qualifying arrangements made at the outset. Confirm the rental term and paperwork before relying on an exemption.

Before you publish a listing

  1. Confirm the property’s jurisdiction and current rules for that address.
  2. Verify primary-residence eligibility and any RSO, affordability, HOA, lease, or ADU restrictions.
  3. Complete the required registrations and permits.
  4. Display the required number on every advertisement.
  5. Understand the applicable annual day or night limit and how you will track it.
  6. Confirm tax collection, reporting, fees, and record retention.
  7. Check insurance and operational responsibilities before accepting guests.

They help you identify questions before spending money on furnishings, marketing, or bookings.

Start with the address

A beautiful furnished home is only one part of a viable rental plan. The first question is whether that exact property is eligible for the rental term you want to offer. Once that is clear, you can evaluate preparation, guest expectations, operating costs, and management.

This article provides general information, not legal advice. Rules and fees change; verify current requirements directly with the responsible city or county and a qualified adviser before listing.
