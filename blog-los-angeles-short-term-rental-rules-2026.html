Los Angeles Short-Term Rental Rules

October 9, 2026
Updated October 27, 2026

Los Angeles City home-sharing rules generally require eligible short-term rental hosts to register the primary residence they use for home sharing. Under the City's program, regular home-sharing registration permits up to 120 days in a calendar year, while extended registration has additional eligibility requirements. Owners should verify their property's jurisdiction and current city rules before accepting bookings or relying on older rental advice.

This distinction matters across the Valley, where a Los Angeles address, an independently incorporated city, and an unincorporated county location can be governed by different programs. The relevant starting point is the authority responsible for the exact property.

Confirm the property and rental arrangement

The City of Los Angeles defines a primary residence as the place a person lives for more than six months of the calendar year and considers their permanent residence. A person may have only one primary residence for the home-sharing program. If the property has multiple dwelling units, the city looks at the specific unit the host actually occupies. Renting a separate unit merely because the host owns the same parcel does not automatically make that unit eligible. Properties outside the City of Los Angeles may be subject to different local requirements.

Registration is separate from listing a property online

A platform may allow an owner to draft a listing, but that does not prove the use is permitted. Los Angeles City Planning maintains a Home-Sharing Application and Registration Portal for applications and renewals. Approved registrations have a defined term and the city requires registration numbers on applicable advertisements. Owners should check the current forms, fees, required documents and eligibility criteria before advertising. Property ownership is not, by itself, sufficient evidence of primary residence.

Regular and extended home sharing have different conditions

The City's ordinary program allows up to 120 days of home sharing per calendar year through a regular registration. Extended home sharing is a separate registration process for eligible hosts who already hold an active regular registration and meet additional conditions. A host should not treat an application for extended registration as approval to book beyond the normal limit. The property's registration status and the applicable calendar year matter.

Rental restrictions may come from other sources

Building covenants, lease agreements, insurance conditions and homeowner association rules can affect whether a particular arrangement is workable even when a city registration might otherwise be available. Owners should not assume that a property advertised by another host represents a valid precedent. Landlords and tenants also have different documentation and permission requirements in some situations. Legal, tax and insurance advisers are appropriate sources for questions that go beyond the city's registration rules.

Longer stays require a different analysis

The city states that its home-sharing registration is not required for long-term rentals, but that does not eliminate other housing or landlord obligations. A furnished thirty-day rental and a weekend rental may raise different legal and insurance questions. Owners should describe the intended length of stay accurately and confirm the applicable local rules rather than treating every rental marketplace category as legally interchangeable.

Use current official guidance

Los Angeles City Planning's Home-Sharing overview and FAQ are the primary sources for the program. Owners should verify changes directly with the city, especially when an online article references a recently discussed ordinance, fee change or state bill. DommyDom's rental-related content helps owners evaluate property use as part of broader real estate planning, but it does not replace individualized legal or tax advice.
