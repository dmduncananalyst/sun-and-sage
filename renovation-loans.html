Financing a Home Purchase and Renovation Together

Some renovation mortgages combine a home purchase with eligible improvements in one loan. The property, work, contractor, and borrower must meet the selected program’s requirements.

Plan the work before the offer

Define the proposed repairs and ask the lender which renovation program fits. A contractor’s scope and cost estimate help the lender evaluate the work and the proposed completed value.

Make room for the process

Renovation financing includes additional review and work completion requirements. Coordinate the financing timeline, permits, contractor availability, and any temporary housing before committing to the property.

California and San Fernando Valley context

For a San Fernando Valley purchase, the lender determines eligibility and loan terms, while the property can add costs such as taxes, HOA dues, insurance, repairs, and appraisal related issues. Compare written lender disclosures with the full cost of the specific home.

Evaluating Work Before Committing

Separate necessary repairs from optional changes so the decision reflects the home’s actual condition. A cosmetic refresh, a failing system, and a structural problem have different implications for disruption, permits, financing, and the order of work. Written estimates should make the scope and exclusions clear rather than treating all improvements as one number.

For California work that requires a licensed contractor, verify the relevant license and discuss permits with the local building authority. Existing alterations may also need review. Leave space for uncertainty before assuming a project can be completed within a particular move or sale timeline, and do not treat any improvement as a guaranteed increase in resale value.

Understanding Written Loan Terms

Compare financing using the written terms for your application and property. The loan amount, repayment period, rate structure, mortgage insurance, lender charges, and cash required at closing work together. A change in one part of the offer can affect the others, so a quoted monthly principal and interest payment is only part of the comparison.

Ask which conditions remain before approval and what changes require a new review. New debt, a change in employment or documented income, or a property issue can affect financing before closing. Use the lender’s current disclosures and explanations rather than relying on a general program description as a promise of eligibility or final terms.
