How Much Down Payment Do You Need to Buy a Home in the San Fernando Valley?

October 22, 2026

Probably less than you think. You do not need 20% down to buy a home in the San Fernando Valley, conventional loans can go as low as 3% down, FHA loans need 3.5%, and VA loans need nothing at all. On a $1 million Valley home, that means the difference between $30,000 and $200,000 at signing. Here's what each path actually looks like in fall 2026.

The down payment menu

Conventional loans, 3% to 5% is realistic. First-time buyers can qualify with as little as 3% down on a Fannie Mae HomeReady-style conventional loan; most lenders are comfortable at 5% for strong profiles. Twenty percent is what kills private mortgage insurance (PMI), not what the loan requires. With less than 20% down, you'll pay PMI, an extra monthly line item that protects the lender, not you. PMI typically costs 0.46% to 1.50% of the loan amount per year depending on your credit score, according to the Urban Institute's Housing Finance Policy Center (via Experian). It drops off automatically once you owe 78% of the home's original value, and you can request cancellation at 80%.

FHA loans, 3.5% down with a 580+ credit score (10% down with scores between 500 and 579). The trade is mortgage insurance premium (MIP): an upfront premium plus an annual one folded into your payment, and if you put down less than 10%, that MIP stays for the life of the loan. In Los Angeles County, the 2026 FHA loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home, the same as the conforming high-cost ceiling, so FHA works on genuinely Valley-priced homes, not just starter prices (CalHFA/CalPLUS program guides, 2026).

VA loans, 0% down, for eligible veterans and active-duty service members. No monthly mortgage insurance; there's a one-time funding fee (which many disabled veterans are exempt from). If you served, this is usually your strongest play.

What that means in San Fernando Valley dollars

The Valley's median single-family price runs about $1.1 million (Southland Regional Association of Realtors, September 2026). On a $1,000,000 purchase:

• 3% down: $30,000 • 3.5% down (FHA): $35,000 • 5% down: $50,000 • 10% down: $100,000 • 20% down: $200,000, no PMI, but that's a lot of savings locked up

And remember the down payment isn't the whole cash picture. Closing costs run another 2, 5% of the purchase price in California, on a million-dollar home that's $20,000, $50,000 more at signing. Lenders and agents who quote you "cash to close" should be folding all of this in, not just the down payment.

California help exists for first-time buyers

If cash at closing is the barrier, look at California's own programs before assuming you're priced out. CalHFA's MyHome Assistance Program offers eligible first-time buyers a deferred-payment second loan of up to 3.5% of the purchase price with a

CalHFA FHA first mortgage (3% with conventional), covering down payment and closing costs with no monthly payment, repaid only when you sell, refinance, or pay off the first mortgage. You need to be a first-time buyer within CalHFA's income limits and complete a homebuyer education course. These programs pair with a CalHFA first mortgage through an approved lender.

The 2026 loan limits matter at Valley prices

One more thing that shapes your down payment strategy: the Federal Housing Finance Agency's 2026 conforming loan limits. The baseline is $832,750, but Los Angeles County is a designated high-cost area, so the ceiling is $1,249,125 for a single-unit home. Keep your loan under that number and you stay in conforming territory with better pricing and looser requirements. Above it, you're in jumbo land, typically 10, 20% down minimum, 700+ credit scores, bigger reserves. At today's national average 30-year fixed of 7.37% (Bankrate, October 22, 2026), loan type and size change your monthly payment meaningfully, get your real numbers from a lender, not from a national average.

The bottom line

The biggest down-payment myth in the Valley is that you need 20%, you don't. You need a loan program that fits your credit and cash, a plan for the insurance costs that come with low down payments, and an honest accounting of closing costs on top. A 3% down payment gets you in the door; 20% gets you the cheapest monthly payment. Everything in between is a strategy conversation worth having with a lender and an agent who knows the local price bands.
