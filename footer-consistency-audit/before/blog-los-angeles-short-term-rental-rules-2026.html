Los Angeles Short Term Rental Rules for Valley Homeowners

October 9, 2026
Updated October 14, 2026

Short term rental requirements in the San Fernando Valley depend on the jurisdiction, the property, and the proposed use. Owners should confirm eligibility and the required approvals before advertising a stay, because a platform listing does not establish that hosting is permitted.

This distinction matters across the Valley, where a Los Angeles address, an independently incorporated city, and an unincorporated county location can be governed by different programs. The relevant starting point is the authority responsible for the exact property.

Identify the governing jurisdiction

The City of Los Angeles and unincorporated Los Angeles County have separate rental programs. A rule that applies in one should not be carried over to the other merely because both locations are within the wider Los Angeles area.

Confirm the address through the appropriate public agency and review that jurisdiction’s current information. Postal naming and a familiar neighborhood label are not always enough to establish which program applies. Where there is uncertainty, resolve it before relying on a calendar, a management proposal, or a projected rental use.

Understand the City of Los Angeles framework

The city’s home sharing program applies to qualifying stays of 30 consecutive days or fewer. It is organized around a host’s primary residence and requires registration. Standard registration permits up to 120 hosting days in a calendar year, with a separate extended home sharing process for eligible hosts seeking more.

Those are starting conditions, not proof that every primary residence qualifies. The city’s current requirements also address property eligibility and restrictions. Review the official program information for the proposed unit and the actual ownership or tenancy arrangement.

A residence subject to restrictions such as rent stabilization or applicable affordability conditions can raise eligibility issues. An accessory unit, investment property, or space within a larger property should not be assumed eligible based only on the fact that it can accommodate a guest.

Other documents can restrict the use

Local registration is only part of the review. A lease, association rules, recorded restrictions, or other governing documents may prohibit or limit the proposed rental. The owner or tenant needs to understand the documents that apply to the property, including any required permissions.

Insurance is another separate issue. A policy for an owner occupied home may not address every proposed rental activity. Explain the intended use to the insurer and review the actual coverage rather than assuming a platform protection program settles every loss or liability question.

County rules need their own review

Unincorporated Los Angeles County administers a separate short term rental certification program through the Treasurer and Tax Collector. Its public information addresses qualifying primary residences and stays of 30 consecutive days or fewer, along with related requirements.

Use the county’s current program information when the property is in its jurisdiction. Registration, tax responsibilities, and restrictions should be evaluated under that program rather than copied from an article about the City of Los Angeles.

A longer stay does not resolve every obligation

A proposal for stays longer than the home sharing threshold requires a separate review of the actual rental arrangement. Being outside a particular home sharing program does not eliminate all housing rules, tenant rights, association restrictions, or insurance considerations.

The appropriate lease, deposit handling, permitted use, and applicable protections should be evaluated for the property and arrangement. Avoid treating a furnished stay or a temporary label as an automatic regulatory exception.

Keep verification connected to the actual use

Before offering a property, establish the proposed length of stay, who occupies the home, and which spaces are being rented. Those details help the relevant agency or adviser assess the arrangement more accurately.

This article focuses on the regulatory foundation. For the separate question of whether the property and owner’s circumstances are suited to hosting, read the Airbnb suitability post.
