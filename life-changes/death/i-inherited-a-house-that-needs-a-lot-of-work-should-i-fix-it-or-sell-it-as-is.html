I inherited a house that needs a lot of work. Should I fix it or sell it as-is?

Whether to repair or sell as-is depends on the home's condition, available cash, timeline and likely return from the improvements. Get realistic estimates before putting estate money into renovations. Title, estate documents, the mortgage, and any court requirements can affect what happens, so confirm legal authority before making a real-estate decision.

Confirm who has authority before making property decisions

The deed, trust, will, court documents, and mortgage records may all affect what can happen next. Being named in a will does not always mean a person can immediately sign a listing agreement or deed. Before accepting an offer, transferring ownership, or spending heavily on repairs, confirm who currently holds title and who has legal authority to act for the property or estate.

Compare repair cost with likely market impact

Obtain estimates for health, safety, structural, system, and cosmetic work. Then compare an as-is price range with the likely price after repairs, the time required, carrying costs, available cash, and the risk of discovering additional problems. The project with the highest projected sale price does not always produce the highest net proceeds.

California and San Fernando Valley context

For an inherited or family home in the San Fernando Valley, the real-estate decision often comes after title, estate authority, condition, carrying costs, and family goals are clarified.
