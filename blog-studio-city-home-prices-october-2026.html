How Much Is a Home in Studio City, California Right Now?

October 22, 2026

As of October 2026, the median sale price in Studio City is roughly $1.73 million, with sellers getting about 97.6% of their asking price, though the picture changes depending on whether you count condos or look at single-family homes only. For single-family homes, the median runs closer to $2.275 million. It's a premium pocket of the San Fernando Valley where presentation and pricing matter enormously.

What the numbers say right now

MLS data for September 2026 (via Realty.com) covers all home types:

• Median sale price: $1,730,000, with a typical range of $1.325 million to $2 million • Homes sold: 21 in September • Sale-to-list ratio: 97.6%, buyers are paying just under asking on average • Paid in cash: a full third of buyers (33.3%) • Median list price: $2,099,000 across 138 homes for sale

Single-family data tells the higher-end story. August 2026 figures (Just Studio City):

• Median single-family sale price: $2,275,000, up 11% year over year • Price per square foot: $937.50, up 2.7% month over month • Average days to sell: 43, up from 27 in July, buyers are taking their time • Active inventory: 165 listings, down 11% from the month before

One honest caveat: the $1.73 million blended median includes condos and townhomes, which pull the number down from the single-family figure. That's why you'll see trackers disagree. The price-per-square-foot figure is the more reliable read on direction, and it's still rising.

What that means if you're buying

Studio City is not the neighborhood for fixer-uppers at a discount. With a third of buyers paying cash and price per square foot climbing, sellers know what they have. That said, 43 average days to sell means well-positioned offers have negotiating room, overpriced listings are sitting while correctly priced ones still move. Have your financing locked in before you fall in love with a Wrightwood Estates view home or a Colfax Meadows flat with Carpenter Community Charter boundary appeal. At these prices, a 20% down payment is $345,000, $455,000, and the 2026 conforming loan ceiling in Los Angeles County is $1,249,125, above that you're into jumbo territory with stricter requirements (FHFA 2026). Talk to a lender early; the neighborhood's price bands make loan type a real strategy question here.

What that means if you're selling

You have a valuable asset in a neighborhood that still appreciates, but the market has opinions about price. With 165 active listings competing for roughly 30 sales a month, overpricing is the single fastest way to go stale. The homes that moved in August were the well-presented ones with real architectural credibility, particularly in the hillside enclaves.

Condition carries unusual weight here. Buyers in this price range compare presentation closely, and the neighborhood's housing mix, median year built 1957, lots of mid-century and Spanish-influenced character homes, rewards updates that

modernize without erasing the charm. Pre-listing inspections, fresh paint, and real listing photography pay disproportionately in seven-figure markets.

Why buyers keep landing here

Studio City sits in the southeast corner of the San Fernando Valley along the Ventura Boulevard corridor, minutes from Hollywood and Universal City but with a genuine neighborhood feel. The entertainment-industry ties run deep, the neighborhood is literally named for the studio lot, and that keeps a steady pipeline of buyers who want Valley space with city access. Tree-lined flat streets, hillside canyon estates, and a walkable commercial strip along Ventura Boulevard give it a range most Valley neighborhoods can't match.

The bottom line

Studio City in October 2026 is a strong, expensive market where the median masks two different realities: a blended $1.73 million across all home types and $2.275 million for single-family homes. Buyers get more negotiating time than they did a year ago but no discounts on the good stuff. Sellers get an appreciating market that punishes wishful pricing. Either way, local data, not national headlines, is what I'll walk you through before you make a move.
