Can the Dodgers Do a Threepeat?

September 28, 2026

Why this run is historically different

The Dodgers are not simply trying to win another World Series. They are trying to become the first Major League team since the 1998 through 2000 Yankees to win three championships in a row. Los Angeles entered October as the back to back defending champion, and no National League team has completed a World Series threepeat.

That puts this roster in a rare position. The Dodgers have championship experience, a roster built to win now, and the pressure of knowing every opponent would love to be the team that ends the run.

The lineup is still loaded

The Dodgers postseason lineup starts with Mookie Betts, Shohei Ohtani, Will Smith and Freddie Freeman, with Teoscar Hernández, Kyle Tucker, Andy Pages, Max Muncy and Tommy Edman adding depth behind them.

October games are not always won with three home runs. Sometimes the difference is one walk, one double, one sacrifice fly or one mistake by the other team. The Dodgers have enough experienced hitters that they do not have to rely on only one person.

The rotation gives them a real chance

The Dodgers carried Tarik Skubal, Blake Snell, Yoshinobu Yamamoto and Tyler Glasnow as their four primary starters for the Division Series. Shohei Ohtani also gives Los Angeles another elite pitching option as a two way player.

That kind of depth can change a short postseason series because an opponent may go several days without seeing a comfortable pitching matchup.

The bullpen still has to finish the job

The Dodgers Division Series bullpen includes Edwin Díaz, Tanner Scott, Roki Sasaki, Alex Vesia, Evan Phillips, Edgardo Henriquez, Kris Bubic and Brock Stewart.

One walk or one misplaced pitch can change an entire playoff game. A third straight championship will require clean late innings when the margin is smallest.

Atlanta is already making them work

The first two games of the Division Series showed why a threepeat is so difficult. Los Angeles won Game 1 against Atlanta, 5 to 3, then the Braves answered in Game 2, 3 to 2. The series moved to Atlanta tied.

Six months of regular season work can come down to a handful of pitches, one defensive mistake or one cold night at the plate.

What would a third straight title mean?

A third championship in a row would move this Dodgers era from a great run into a piece of baseball history. It would also make Los Angeles the first National League club to win three straight World Series titles.

The Dodgers have the lineup, the pitching and the championship experience. What they still need is another full October where the biggest players stay healthy, the bullpen survives close games and the offense delivers when one run matters more than ten.

So, can they actually do it?

Yes, they can. But being talented enough to win and actually winning three championships in a row are two very different things.

That is why this postseason is worth watching. A threepeat is not expected. It would be historic, and the Dodgers still have to earn every step of it.
