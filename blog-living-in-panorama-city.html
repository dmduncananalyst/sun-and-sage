Panorama City, Built for the Heart of the Valley

October 21, 2026

Panorama City has a story most Valley neighborhoods cannot match. Before it was a neighborhood, it was an idea, a whole community planned on paper and then built for the families who came home from World War II ready to start their lives. Living here today means living inside that idea, updated by generations of new arrivals who made it their own. It is busy, diverse, practical, and right in the middle of the San Fernando Valley, and for a lot of people, that combination is exactly the point. This is not a sleepy corner tucked away from everything. Panorama City puts you in the flow of Valley life, with shopping, medical care, schools, and recreation woven into the neighborhood itself, the way its planners intended from the very beginning. If you like the feeling that life is happening all around you, and that everything you need is close, this neighborhood deserves a real look.

Where Panorama City Sits in the Valley

Panorama City sits in the heart of the San Fernando Valley, southeast of Mission Hills and close to North Hills, Van Nuys, and Arleta, about nineteen miles northwest of Downtown Los Angeles according to local guides. Van Nuys Boulevard runs through it as the main commercial spine, with Roscoe Boulevard and Woodman Avenue carrying much of the everyday traffic across the neighborhood. That central position is the thing residents rely on most. You are not driving in from the edge of the Valley for every errand, appointment, and family gathering. You are starting from the middle, which shortens trips in every direction. Local guides also note the neighborhood's solid transit connections along its main boulevards, and the East San Fernando Valley Light Rail project now in the works along Van Nuys Boulevard is planned to connect the Van Nuys station with the Sylmar and San Fernando Metrolink station, with fourteen new stations along a nine point two mile route. That is a future change, not a current amenity, but it tells you where the city sees this corridor going.

The Valley's First Planned Community

Panorama City is known as the San Fernando Valley's first planned community. In 1948, residential developer Fritz B. Burns and industrialist Henry J. Kaiser developed it as such, with a master plan created by the architectural firm Wurdeman and Becket. Kaiser history tells the story in more detail. Kaiser and Burns formed Kaiser Community Homes after the war, and Panorama City became its largest development, with three thousand homes built between 1947 and 1952. The plan was never just houses. It made room for schools, churches, shopping, and recreation, a self contained community where returning veterans and their families could put down roots. The blueprint even designated space for a Kaiser Permanente clinic and hospital, which was completed in 1962, and Kaiser Permanente Panorama City Medical Center still anchors the heart of the neighborhood today. You can feel that planned beginning in the way commercial and residential blocks are organized, even as the neighborhood has grown denser and more urban over the decades. Panorama City now has the highest population density in the Valley, and more than half of its residents were born abroad, the majority from Mexico. A community that began as a postwar plan became, over time, one of the most international places in the Valley, and that is a big part of its character now.

The Homes and the Streets

Today Panorama City is a mixture of single family homes and low rise apartment buildings, and that mix shapes who lives here and how the neighborhood feels. There are original postwar homes on residential streets where families have been rooted for years, and there are apartment communities that give renters and first time buyers a way into a central Valley location. For buyers, that range matters. Some people come to Panorama City for a single family home with a yard in the middle of the Valley. Others start in an apartment here because the location makes work, school, and family logistics easier, and then decide they want to stay in the neighborhood when they are ready to buy. Either way, you are choosing a place where daily life is close at hand rather than a place where every need starts with a long drive. The honest trade off is density. Streets are livelier, parking takes a little more thought, and the pace is more urban than in the Valley's quieter corners. Plenty of residents would not trade that energy away. It just helps to know, going in, that Panorama City feels like a small city, because that is what it was designed to be.

Shopping, Food, and Everyday Life on Van Nuys Boulevard

Shopping is a genuine strong suit here. Panorama Mall, The Plant Shopping Center, and Plaza Del Valle together cover everything from daily essentials to major national retailers, and each one has its own role in local life. Plaza Del Valle, on Van Nuys Boulevard, is the one people describe with the most affection. Local guides describe families wandering an outdoor pedestrian plaza with over one hundred shops and restaurants in open air stalls, a brightly colored center with murals and vibrant sculptures that has been a community staple since 1960. To residents, it is more than a shopping center. It is a place where neighbors gather, eat together at outdoor tables, and catch up, and where you can find places like Ponchos Taqueria right inside the plaza. The Plant tells a different Valley story. Panorama City was once home to a large General Motors assembly plant, and when that plant closed, the site was eventually replaced by The Plant, a large shopping center with stores, restaurants, and a movie theater. Where generations of Valley residents once built cars, their grandchildren now do their shopping and catch a film. That kind of reinvention says a lot about this neighborhood and its staying power. Food follows the community. Along Van Nuys Boulevard and Woodman Avenue you will find Latin groceries and specialty foods, Filipino cooking, taquerias, and the everyday mix of markets and family restaurants that reflect the many cultures who call Panorama City home. Grocery options, from Vallarta Supermarket to the larger stores near the mall, keep the weekly routine close to home.

Parks, Recreation, and Family Life

Panorama City has two recreational centers, a senior center, and three high schools, including Panorama High School, along with a mix of public and charter schools for younger students. For a dense neighborhood, having that infrastructure inside its own borders matters, and it goes back to the original plan, which treated recreation and schools as essentials, not extras. The Sepulveda Recreation Center anchors outdoor life in and around the neighborhood, with baseball diamonds, indoor basketball courts, a children's play area, and a seasonal pool, among its sports facilities, tennis courts, and gymnasiums. The Panorama Recreation Center is another local favorite, with its own gymnasium, baseball diamond, and play area, and both centers host the leagues, classes, and after school hours that give local kids a place to be. For a bigger day outside, Lake Balboa Park sits about five miles southwest, with golf, a lake, a Japanese garden, and room to spread out. But week to week, it is the neighborhood centers that carry family life here, and they are busy in the best way.

What It Feels Like to Call It Home

Panorama City works best for people who want to be in the middle of the Valley and in the middle of life. It has deep roots, a real history, medical care in the neighborhood, shopping that doubles as community gathering space, and a diversity that shows up in its food, its festivals, and its streets. It is practical in a way that planned communities were meant to be, and human in a way no plan could have predicted. If that sounds like your kind of Valley, I would love to drive Van Nuys Boulevard with you and walk a few of these residential streets together, because Panorama City makes the most sense when you see the plan and the people at the same time.
