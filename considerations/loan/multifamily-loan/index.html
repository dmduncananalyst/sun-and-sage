Multifamily Loan

Financing for two to four residential units may follow residential mortgage rules. Buildings with five or more units generally require commercial multifamily underwriting.

Eligibility and property requirements

Owner occupancy, rental income, unit count, condition and reserves determine the financing route. Commercial lenders evaluate operating income and property value.

Advantages

May support an owner occupied small multifamily purchase or a larger rental investment.

Disadvantages and risks

Operating expenses, tenant obligations, deferred maintenance and vacancy can reduce returns.

Interest rates and borrowing costs

Compare residential and commercial terms, amortization and any balloon maturity.

Practical considerations

A buyer considering a duplex should compare living in one unit with buying the entire property as an investment, including different financing requirements.

Financing structure

Two to four unit properties can qualify for residential programs depending on occupancy and underwriting. Five or more units generally use commercial analysis of operating income, value and debt coverage. Agency and private products differ.

Underwriting and documentation

For smaller properties, provide borrower financials, leases and appraisal rent schedules. For larger buildings, provide rent roll, operating statements, capital expenditure history, leases and entity financials. Lenders may adjust income and expenses to stabilized levels.

Illustrative financing calculation

A building with $180,000 annual effective income and $75,000 operating expenses has $105,000 net operating income. With $84,000 annual debt service, DSCR is 1.25. At an assumed 5% capitalization rate, the illustrative income based value is $2.1 million.

Transaction planning and risk

Inspect units, deferred maintenance, legal unit count and tenant obligations. Los Angeles rent restrictions and seismic retrofit requirements can affect expenses and future income. Review replacement reserves, recourse and maturity before choosing financing.

Loan options

Conventional 2 to 4 Unit: Residential conforming mortgage with unit count limits and occupancy specific underwriting. FHA 2 to 4 Unit: FHA insured residential mortgage, county unit count limits, mortgage insurance. VA 2 to 4 Unit: VA guaranteed residential mortgage; zero down may be possible if entitlement and underwriting permit. FHA 223(f): HUD insured acquisition or refinance, term up to 35 years subject to property life and program limits. FHA 221(d)(4): HUD insured construction and permanent financing, permanent term up to 40 years; statutory limits vary by unit and location. FHA 223(a)(7): Refinance to improve loan economics; term extension constrained by original program and remaining useful life. FHA 241(a): Supplemental insured financing for eligible improvements or additions. Fannie Mae Multifamily: Agency financing through approved lenders; fixed and variable structures, term and amortization depend on product. Freddie Mac Multifamily: Agency financing with product dependent maturity, amortization and prepayment terms. Bank Multifamily: Portfolio commercial mortgage, often amortization longer than maturity; possible guarantees. Investment Bridge: Short maturity, possible interest only payments, draw or holdback provisions. Construction Only: Separate temporary construction note; payoff or refinance required at maturity.

Loan structure and qualification details

Two to four unit residential financing and five or more unit commercial financing are separate markets. FHA, VA and conventional owner occupied loans may allow rental income; agency and HUD commercial programs focus on property income, sponsor and regulatory requirements.

Financing and cost analysis

A building with $300,000 annual NOI and illustrative 1.25 DSCR supports $240,000 annual debt service. A $30,000 reduction in NOI reduces that capacity to $216,000. Appraisal and leverage limits can further constrain borrowing.

Benefits, limitations and lender review

Obtain legal unit count, trailing operating statements, rent roll and capital needs assessment. Compare term, amortization, balloon, recourse, reserves, affordability restrictions and prepayment terms.

Residential versus commercial multifamily

Two to four units can qualify for residential mortgages, including eligible owner occupied FHA and VA options. Five or more units generally require commercial underwriting. HUD multifamily insurance is distinct from a single family FHA loan.

Qualification, documentation and lending considerations

Commercial lenders examine historical rent collections, NOI, DSCR, property condition, sponsor liquidity, management and legal unit count. Maximum leverage is not sufficient if income cannot support the debt. Compare recourse, reserve and prepayment provisions.

Financing example and practical risks

A building with $300,000 NOI and assumed 1.25 DSCR can support $240,000 annual debt service. If NOI falls to $250,000, capacity falls to $200,000. This income test may produce a smaller mortgage than the appraisal based limit.

Residential versus commercial underwriting

Two to four unit homes can use residential mortgage programs, subject to occupancy and unit rules. Five or more units generally require commercial multifamily analysis. Compare net operating income, debt coverage, reserves, property condition and sponsor liquidity. A 30 year amortization with a 10 year maturity still creates a balloon. Agency, HUD, bank and bridge loans serve different property stabilization and development needs.
