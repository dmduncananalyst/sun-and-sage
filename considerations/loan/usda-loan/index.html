USDA Loan

USDA housing programs support eligible borrowers buying primary homes in designated rural areas. Guaranteed loans and direct loans have different rules.

Eligibility and property requirements

Property location and household income must qualify. Eligible guaranteed loans can offer zero down financing. Most urban San Fernando Valley addresses are unlikely to qualify, so check the exact address.

Advantages

Potential zero down payment for eligible buyers outside major urban areas.

Disadvantages and risks

Geographic and income restrictions and applicable guarantee fees limit suitability.

Interest rates and borrowing costs

Compare rate, upfront guarantee fee and annual fee with FHA or conventional alternatives.

Practical considerations

A buyer considering a move beyond central Los Angeles can check the address before assuming USDA financing is available.

Financing structure

The guaranteed program uses approved private lenders, while the direct program serves qualifying lower income applicants through Rural Development. The two programs have distinct income, repayment and application rules.

Underwriting and documentation

Guaranteed program eligibility considers adjusted household income, which can include income from household members who are not borrowers, separately from repayment income. Check the exact address and household size against official eligibility tools. The home must be an eligible principal residence.

Illustrative financing calculation

For an illustrative $400,000 purchase with no down payment and an assumed 1% financed guarantee fee, the initial balance would be $404,000. An annual fee may also apply. Confirm current fee schedules and whether appraisal supports financed costs. Zero down does not mean zero cash at closing.

Transaction planning and risk

Do not assume a property qualifies merely because it is outside Los Angeles city limits. Verify address eligibility before an offer and ask about processing time, water and septic requirements, and any repair conditions. Direct program payment assistance may carry recapture obligations.

Loan options

USDA Guaranteed: Approved lender originates guaranteed mortgage; eligible financing can have zero down, with guarantee fees. USDA Direct: USDA lends directly; payment assistance may reduce effective payment and be subject to subsidy recapture.

Loan structure and qualification details

USDA guaranteed loans are originated through participating lenders, while Direct loans are made by Rural Development. Both are intended for eligible primary homes, not vacation or rental investments. The geographic boundary and income test are independent of credit approval.

Financing and cost analysis

Zero down does not eliminate costs. For a $350,000 purchase, an illustrative 1% financed guarantee fee produces $353,500 principal. Direct payment assistance works differently and may be subject to repayment of subsidy upon exit.

Benefits, limitations and lender review

Check the exact property address using USDA eligibility tools before paying for inspections. Report household composition and income accurately; eligibility income may differ from income used to calculate repayment ability. Ask about processing time and funding availability.

Rural housing financing and program selection

USDA Rural Development provides two distinct Section 502 purchase pathways. Guaranteed financing is originated by participating private lenders; Direct financing is funded by USDA for eligible low and very low income applicants. Section 504 is a separate repair program for existing homeowners. USDA financing is not intended for a vacation home or an income producing investment property.

Qualification, documentation and lending considerations

Check the actual street address on the USDA eligibility map before making an offer. Rural eligibility is not determined by the mailing city alone. The agency considers household income for program eligibility, which can include income from adults who are not borrowers, while repayment underwriting uses qualifying income.

Guaranteed loans are limited to a 30 year fixed term and households generally cannot exceed 115% of median household income under USDA calculations. Direct loans generally have a 33 year term, with 38 years available to qualifying very low income applicants. Direct applicants must demonstrate inability to obtain suitable credit elsewhere.

Financing example and practical risks

A $350,000 eligible purchase with zero down can still require funds for appraisal, inspections, prepaid taxes, insurance and closing expenses. If a hypothetical 1% guarantee fee is financed, principal becomes $353,500. At an assumed 6.5% for 30 years, principal and interest is approximately $2,234, before annual guarantee charges and property expenses.

Direct borrowers instead should examine payment assistance and subsidy recapture: a reduced monthly payment today can create a repayment obligation when the home is sold or occupancy ends. Do not confuse either program with a grant. Compare FHA and conventional financing when the address or household income fails USDA eligibility.

Additional financing options

USDA Section 504 Home Repair Loan: Direct USDA repair financing for eligible rural homeowners.

USDA Refinance: Refinancing options for existing eligible USDA borrowers.

CalHFA USDA Loan: California program financing using USDA eligible rural housing mortgages.

Program selection and eligibility

USDA financing serves eligible owner occupants, not investors acquiring a rental portfolio. Section 502 Guaranteed is originated by approved private lenders, while Section 502 Direct is an agency loan for households with lower incomes and limited access to other credit.

Section 504 is repair financing for existing homeowners, not a purchase mortgage. Existing Section 502 borrowers may also qualify for a refinance. CalHFA USDA combines an eligible guaranteed first mortgage with California program rules; it is not a third federal Section 502 loan.

Household income and repayment ability

Start with the income of the household, including applicable income from nonborrowing adults. The guaranteed program generally applies a ceiling of 115% of the applicable median income under USDA rules. Direct uses separate low and very low income limits. Allowable deductions can affect adjusted household income.

Repayment income, by contrast, is the stable income available to the obligated borrowers. For example, an adult relative living in the home may affect the household eligibility calculation without being obligated to pay the mortgage. The applicable limit depends on the location and

household composition. A high credit score does not override an income or address restriction.

Property and appraisal requirements

Enter the actual property address in the USDA eligibility tool; a rural mailing address alone does not qualify the parcel. The residence must be suitable for year round occupancy and meet applicable safety, sanitation, access and utility requirements.

An appraiser evaluates market value and program property conditions; an independent inspection evaluates the condition of the home in greater detail. Verify private road maintenance, well water, septic permits, flood hazards, insurability and legal access before removing purchase contingencies. Manufactured housing and condominiums require product-specific review.

Rates, fees and cash at closing

For the guaranteed program, the January 2026 USDA fee schedule lists a 1% upfront guarantee charge and a 0.35% annual charge based on the average scheduled unpaid principal. The first charge may be financed if eligible; the second is typically collected monthly. A 30 year fixed mortgage at a hypothetical 6.5% on $353,500 costs about $2,234 in principal and interest.

On the starting balance, 0.35% corresponds to roughly $103 per month before the actual scheduled balance calculation. With $360 in taxes and $160 in insurance, the illustrative total is approximately $2,857. No down payment does not mean no cash: inspection, deposits, prepaid expenses and uncovered settlement costs may remain.

Application, closing and servicing

For Guaranteed, contact a participating lender, provide income and household information, obtain preliminary underwriting, then identify a property. The lender orders appraisal and verifies credit, assets, debts, title, insurance and program eligibility. The lender submits the required guarantee package to USDA.

Direct applicants instead apply through Rural Development or an eligible packager and await agency eligibility and funding review. Before closing, compare cash required and payment obligations. After closing, maintain the property and required insurance, pay taxes and contact the servicer promptly if payments become difficult. A sale normally requires loan payoff; Direct assistance may also trigger subsidy recapture.

Comparing financing

An FHA loan may be available in urban areas and to households above USDA income ceilings, but requires qualifying borrower funds and mortgage insurance. Conventional financing may provide more occupancy options and potentially cancellable private mortgage insurance.

VA can offer eligible military borrowers zero down without a monthly mortgage insurance charge. USDA Direct may be especially relevant when suitable private credit is unavailable, but funding and subsidy obligations make its process distinct. Compare the actual total housing payment and cash needed rather than selecting solely by down payment.
