VA Loan

VA purchase loans are offered by lenders and guaranteed by the Department of Veterans Affairs for eligible veterans, service members and certain surviving spouses.

Eligibility and property requirements

A Certificate of Eligibility establishes entitlement information, not final credit approval. Eligible buyers with full entitlement may buy without a down payment if the lender approves and the price does not exceed appraised value. Occupancy rules apply.

Advantages

No monthly mortgage insurance and potential zero down financing.

Disadvantages and risks

A funding fee may apply unless exempt. Appraisal and occupancy requirements apply.

Interest rates and borrowing costs

Compare lender rates, funding fee, closing costs and any points. VA does not set a single retail mortgage rate.

Practical considerations

A veteran relocating to the San Fernando Valley can obtain a Certificate of Eligibility and discuss purchase financing before touring homes.

Financing structure

VA entitlement is the amount of guaranty available, not cash paid to the buyer. Full entitlement and remaining entitlement are treated differently. A borrower with an outstanding VA loan or prior loss may need an entitlement calculation before another purchase.

Underwriting and documentation

Obtain a Certificate of Eligibility, then provide income, assets and debt documentation. VA underwriting considers residual income, meaning money remaining after specified obligations and living expenses. The lender may set credit standards. The appraisal establishes reasonable value and evaluates minimum property requirements.

Illustrative financing calculation

For a hypothetical $600,000 purchase at appraised value with no down payment, the base balance is $600,000. If a 2.15% funding fee applies, financing it adds $12,900. Actual fee depends on use history, down payment and exemption status. Closing costs and prepaid expenses remain even when no down payment is required.

Transaction planning and risk

Review the VA escape clause and appraisal process with the agent and lender. Confirm occupancy plans for active duty relocation, entitlement restoration after a prior sale, and whether a funding fee exemption applies. Compare offers from multiple VA lenders.

Loan options

VA Purchase: Potential zero down with full entitlement and adequate appraisal; no monthly mortgage insurance; funding fee unless exempt. VA IRRRL: Generally no equity cash withdrawal; allowable costs may be financed, subject to VA rules. VA Cash Out Refinance: Can replace VA or non VA mortgage; cash proceeds and maximum LTV depend on current VA and lender rules.

Loan structure and qualification details

VA guarantees part of eligible loans made by private lenders. Eligibility is based on service and entitlement rather than first time buyer status. Purchase loans, IRRRL refinances and cash out refinances have different occupancy and underwriting rules. A COE is evidence of eligibility, not approval.

Financing and cost analysis

A $650,000 purchase with zero down and an illustrative 2.15% funding fee creates $13,975 in fees if applicable. Financing the fee raises principal to $663,975. A funding fee exemption can substantially change the comparison. Taxes, insurance and closing charges remain payable.

Benefits, limitations and lender review

Ask the lender to calculate entitlement, residual income and any required down payment. Obtain VA appraisal and minimum property requirement review. If an existing VA loan remains outstanding, verify remaining entitlement before relying on zero down.

Entitlement and financing mechanics

The VA guarantee protects the participating lender, not the borrower from default. Eligibility is established through a Certificate of Eligibility. Full entitlement does not guarantee approval of any price; partial entitlement can require a down payment. The appraisal must support the transaction.

Qualification, documentation and lending considerations

Residual income measures funds available after major obligations and differs from the debt ratio. Family size and region matter. Confirm funding fee exemption and whether seller concessions or repairs comply with VA rules. VA purchase financing normally requires occupancy, unlike a passive rental acquisition.

Financing example and practical risks

A $750,000 purchase with no down payment and a hypothetical applicable 2.15% fee results in a $766,125 loan if the fee is financed. A buyer exempt from the fee would begin at $750,000. Compare payment, cash to close and remaining entitlement before making an offer.

Additional financing options

Native American Direct Loan (NADL): VA direct financing for eligible Native American veterans and spouses on federal trust land.

CalHFA VA Loan: California state program first mortgage using VA financing.

Entitlement, appraisal and residual income

The COE shows eligibility and entitlement, not the maximum affordable purchase price. Remaining entitlement matters when a previous VA loan has not been restored. VA residual income measures funds remaining after taxes, major debts and household costs; a debt ratio alone does not establish qualification.

A purchase above the appraised reasonable value can require cash or a price reduction. Ask the lender to verify funding fee exemption and whether the veteran can restore entitlement after payoff.
