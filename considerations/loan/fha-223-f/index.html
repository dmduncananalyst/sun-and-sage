FHA 223(f)

Financing structure

HUD insured acquisition or refinance, term up to 35 years subject to property life and program limits.

Borrower and property eligibility

Existing apartment properties generally with five or more units; substantial rehabilitation is not eligible.

Underwriting and application

MAP lender reviews operating statements, rent roll, appraisal, environmental and physical assessment; critical repairs required before endorsement.

Program details, costs and financing example

HUD insured financing for existing multifamily properties generally requires at least five units and is designed for acquisition or refinance rather than substantial rehabilitation. A MAP approved lender evaluates statutory per unit limits, value, eligible costs and debt service coverage; affordability classification affects leverage and coverage. The permanent term can reach 35 years subject to remaining economic life. For $240,000 annual NOI and assumed 1.20 coverage, annual debt service cannot exceed $200,000; at 6% over 35 years this supports about $2.92 million before other limits.

On $4 million value at assumed 80% LTV, the value ceiling is $3.2 million, so income controls in this example. Review mortgage insurance, replacement reserves, physical needs assessment, environmental review, appraisal, repair escrow, regulatory agreement and distributions. Critical repairs generally must be completed before endorsement. The lender must confirm current HUD underwriting ratios and insurance premiums for the specific affordability and property classification.

Loan sizing and eligible costs The lender calculates separate ceilings based on statutory mortgage limits, eligible acquisition or refinancing costs, appraised value and income available for debt service. The lowest applicable ceiling governs.

Market rate, affordable and subsidized properties may have different underwriting standards. A borrower should not apply the same leverage assumption to all properties. The appraisal must reflect the property and its legally permitted use, and the lender must reconcile historical income with supported market assumptions.

Operating income and reserves Gross potential rent is not net operating income. Deduct vacancy, concessions, management, utilities paid by the owner, property taxes, insurance, maintenance

and other recurring expenses. Replacement reserve deposits may also affect underwriting.

For example, $420,000 potential annual rent less $30,000 vacancy and $150,000 operating expenses produces $240,000 NOI before any additional reserve adjustment. If insurance increases by $20,000, NOI falls to $220,000 and at an illustrative 1.20 coverage requirement allowable annual debt service falls from $200,000 to $183,333.

Property review and closing A HUD approved multifamily lender coordinates appraisal, environmental review, physical condition assessment and underwriting. The borrower supplies ownership records, rent roll, leases, operating statements, capital expenditure history and repair estimates. The lender identifies critical repairs, noncritical repairs and required reserves. HUD

review and endorsement are distinct from an ordinary bank

closing. The borrower must understand the regulatory agreement, reporting obligations, distribution restrictions, insurance premiums and any prepayment provisions before committing.

Permanent financing and property eligibility HUD Section 223(f) is designed for existing rental housing rather than major redevelopment. Its published maximum term is 35 years. Mortgage sizing involves multiple independent limitations; a strong appraisal does not compensate for inadequate operating income.

Critical repairs precede endorsement, whereas approved noncritical repairs may be handled afterward. The investor should obtain a physical needs review, estimate replacement reserve deposits and confirm the current HUD insurance premium and coverage thresholds with a MAP lender. The regulatory agreement can constrain distributions even when the property produces cash.

Loan structure and qualification details

A stabilized property's historical operating results must support the loan. The lender distinguishes deferred maintenance from substantial rehabilitation and identifies eligible repair escrows.

Financing and cost analysis

At $200,000 annual debt service and $240,000 NOI, coverage is 1.20. If vacancy reduces NOI by $30,000, coverage falls to 1.05. Underwriting should test insurance and tax increases as well.

Benefits, limitations and lender review

Long amortization and insured permanent debt may be attractive; processing, regulatory obligations and repair requirements are tradeoffs. Compare agency and bank alternatives.

Existing apartment financing

Section 223(f) provides HUD insured financing for qualifying existing multifamily properties, generally five or more units. It supports acquisition or refinancing with limited rehabilitation, not substantial redevelopment. A MAP lender determines applicable underwriting limits.

Qualification, documentation and lending considerations

Review statutory per unit limits, appraisal, eligible costs, income coverage, physical needs assessment, environmental review and replacement reserves. Regulatory agreements can affect distributions and ongoing compliance.

Financing example and practical risks

With $300,000 NOI and assumed 1.25 coverage, annual debt service cannot exceed $240,000. At 6% over 35 years that supports about $3.51 million before other ceilings. A $4 million appraisal at assumed 80% permits $3.2 million, so value controls in this scenario.

Existing property and regulatory obligations

The program is for eligible existing multifamily properties rather than major redevelopment. Loan sizing considers statutory limits, valuation, eligible costs and income coverage. The MAP lender assesses physical needs, environmental matters, repairs, replacement reserves and regulatory restrictions. A lower NOI or insurance increase can constrain debt even when property value is unchanged.
