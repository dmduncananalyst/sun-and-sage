First Time Homebuyer Loans

Buying your first home does not require one particular mortgage. Conventional, FHA, VA and eligible assistance programs can all provide a route into ownership. Some programs define a first time buyer as someone who has not owned a principal residence during the preceding three years.

Eligibility and property requirements

Compare credit, income, debts, savings and the intended property. Some conventional purchase programs allow 3% down and FHA can allow 3.5% down. Eligible VA and USDA borrowers may qualify without a down payment. Assistance programs can impose separate income and education requirements.

Advantages

A smaller initial contribution can preserve savings for moving and repairs.

Disadvantages and risks

A smaller down payment can increase borrowing costs and leave less equity. Assistance may need repayment.

Interest rates and borrowing costs

Compare actual mortgage offers rather than assuming a first buyer discount. Include insurance, points and assistance repayment.

Practical considerations

A renter buying a $600,000 home with 3% down contributes $18,000 toward the price, before closing costs or assistance. Ask a lender which programs accept the property and your circumstances.

Financing structure

A first purchase is a transaction category rather than a single loan. Compare conventional, FHA, VA and USDA eligibility separately. A prior ownership interest, including an interest held with a spouse, can affect eligibility for assistance even if the buyer has never signed a mortgage. Program definitions differ.

Underwriting and documentation

Lenders examine recurring gross income, monthly obligations, credit history and verified funds. Salaried buyers may provide pay statements and W-2 forms; self employed buyers may need tax returns and business records. Gift funds require documentation under the selected program. Obtain a preapproval before making an offer, but keep financing and appraisal protections in mind.

Illustrative financing calculation

Budget separately for earnest money, down payment, inspections, lender charges, title and escrow, prepaid insurance and tax reserves. Earnest money generally credits toward closing rather than adding to the purchase price. A $600,000 purchase with 3% down and assumed closing costs of $15,000 requires $33,000 before credits; a $5,000 deposit already paid leaves $28,000. These are illustrations, not estimates of actual charges.

Transaction planning and risk

Compare mortgage insurance duration, assistance repayment and the cash remaining after closing. In Los Angeles County, obtain an insurance quote early and verify the property tax estimate based on the purchase rather than relying on the seller's existing tax bill.

Loan options

Conventional 97: Fixed rate conforming financing with mortgage insurance above 80% LTV; conforming county limits apply. HomeReady: Conforming mortgage with reduced mortgage insurance requirements in qualifying cases; gifts and eligible assistance may fund closing. Home Possible: Eligible purchases may allow 3% down; private mortgage insurance and conforming limits apply. FHA 203(b): FHA insurance includes upfront and annual premiums; local FHA limits depend on unit count. VA Purchase: Potential zero down with full entitlement and adequate appraisal; no monthly mortgage insurance; funding fee unless exempt. USDA Guaranteed: Approved lender originates guaranteed mortgage; eligible financing can have zero down, with guarantee fees. CalHFA FHA: FHA first mortgage through approved CalHFA lender; eligible assistance is separate debt. CalHFA Conventional: Conforming first mortgage; possible mortgage insurance and compatible subordinate assistance. MyHome: Deferred payment junior loan, not an automatic grant; amount and repayment triggers depend on current program.

Loan structure and qualification details

A first purchase involves three separate tests: eligibility for the loan, ability to make the payment, and sufficient funds to close. Conventional 97 is not the same as an income restricted affordable mortgage.

FHA can accommodate different credit profiles but adds government insurance. VA requires military eligibility, and USDA requires an eligible location and household income. CalHFA assistance can be combined only with compatible financing.

Financing and cost analysis

On a $600,000 home, 3% down is $18,000, 3.5% is $21,000 and 20% is $120,000. Add escrow, title, prepaid insurance and taxes separately. A $12,000 closing budget would make the first two cash requirements $30,000 and $33,000 before credits. Compare the total payment and remaining savings, not just down payment.

Benefits, limitations and lender review

Obtain a lender review before making an offer. Identify whether any borrower owned a principal residence in the relevant lookback period; request income limits by address, gift documentation and education requirements. California buyers should obtain an actual insurance quote and check special assessments before selecting a property.

Choosing a financing program

Start with the purchase price and the amount of savings that can remain untouched after closing. A first time buyer definition often looks back three years, but some programs use different ownership tests. A spouse's ownership history can affect assistance eligibility. A buyer who previously owned a home may still qualify for FHA or VA financing even if an assistance program is unavailable.

Qualification, documentation and lending considerations

Compare a conventional mortgage with cancellable PMI against FHA with upfront and continuing insurance. For a household with variable commissions, ask the lender to establish eligible income before using an online affordability estimate. A preapproval based on incomplete income information is not a commitment.

Financing example and practical risks

For a $600,000 purchase, a $30,000 savings balance and $18,000 down leave $12,000 for closing and reserves. If closing consumes $12,000, no emergency savings remain. Negotiate eligible seller credits or consider a lower price rather than assuming a zero reserve budget is sustainable.

Additional financing options

HomeOne: Freddie Mac low down payment conventional mortgage without an income cap.

Eligibility and cash planning

A first time buyer designation often uses a three year ownership lookback, but the definition and exceptions vary by program. Separate the loan from assistance: an eligible conventional or FHA mortgage may be paired with a junior lien that becomes due later.

Compare a $500,000 purchase at 3% down ($15,000) and 3.5% down ($17,500), then add the actual mortgage insurance, escrow, settlement expenses and reserve requirement. A smaller down payment is not automatically the cheapest five year option.
