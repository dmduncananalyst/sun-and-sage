FHA 221(d)(4)

Financing structure

HUD insured construction and permanent financing, permanent term up to 40 years; statutory limits vary by unit and location.

Borrower and property eligibility

New construction or substantial rehabilitation of eligible rental or cooperative housing with at least five units.

Underwriting and application

MAP lender submits market study, plans, zoning, budget, sponsor qualifications and underwriting for HUD commitment.

Program details, costs and financing example

HUD insured construction and substantial rehabilitation financing combines construction and permanent debt for eligible multifamily projects. The permanent term may reach 40 years, separate from the construction period. Sizing considers eligible development cost, statutory limits, projected stabilized income and applicable coverage.

On $10 million eligible cost at assumed 85% leverage, the cost ceiling is $8.5 million; $650,000 stabilized NOI at assumed 1.20 coverage supports $541,667 annual debt service, or roughly $8.2 million at 6% over 40 years. Review architectural plans, cost certification, contractor bonding, wage requirements, environmental clearance, initial and final endorsement, construction draws and operating deficit reserves. Delays in lease up can increase required sponsor cash. Confirm current HUD thresholds and applicable affordability classification with a MAP lender.

Construction funding and completion The insured mortgage combines construction and permanent financing for eligible multifamily new construction or substantial rehabilitation. The lender reviews architectural plans, specifications, construction contract, contractor qualifications, permits, environmental findings, market demand and projected operating income.

Construction advances are tied to approved costs and inspected progress. A borrower needs sufficient liquidity for costs excluded from the mortgage, changes in scope and delays in lease up.

Budget and stabilization A $10 million development with $8 million in assumed financing requires $2 million of equity before any ineligible costs or overruns. A 5% increase in project cost adds $500,000; it does not automatically increase the mortgage.

Underwriting must account for interest during construction, operating deficits, initial occupancy, replacement reserves and the timing of permanent amortization. Review completion guarantees and the conditions for final endorsement.

Development financing and construction exposure HUD describes eligibility for rental and cooperative developments of at least five units, with permanent financing potentially extending to 40 years. The developer must establish market demand, site control, zoning, construction feasibility

and sponsor capacity. At a hypothetical $12 million project cost, $9 million in debt leaves $3 million of equity before ineligible costs and overruns. Interest during construction, professional fees, contingency and lease up deficits must be reflected in the project budget. The MAP lender submits the underwriting package for HUD review and firm

commitment; approval is not simply a residential mortgage prequalification.

Loan structure and qualification details

Development underwriting includes market absorption, construction budget, contractor capacity and operating deficit funding. The permanent amortization period is separate from construction.

Financing and cost analysis

If 100 units are projected to lease at ten per month, a five unit monthly absorption pace doubles the lease up period. The sponsor must fund resulting operating deficits and carrying costs.

Benefits, limitations and lender review

Long term insured financing can reduce takeout uncertainty, but HUD review and construction compliance are significant. Verify wage, bonding and cost certification requirements.

Multifamily development financing

Section 221(d)(4) combines insured construction and permanent financing for qualifying rental and cooperative development of at least five units. Permanent amortization can extend to 40 years, apart from the construction period.

Qualification, documentation and lending considerations

A MAP lender evaluates market study, zoning, architectural plans, costs, contractor, sponsor capacity, environmental clearance and stabilized income. Construction advances require documentation; cost certification and final endorsement follow completion requirements.

Financing example and practical risks

A $12 million project at hypothetical 80% eligible cost financing requires $2.4 million equity. A 7% cost overrun adds $840,000; loan proceeds do not automatically rise. Lease up delays can also require operating deficit funding.

Development budget and HUD endorsement

Construction and substantial rehabilitation financing requires a detailed market and development package. Include design, permits, construction, interest, contingencies, operating deficits and lease up expenses. A $15 million project with a 5% cost overrun requires $750,000 additional funding unless an approved source covers it. Review cost certification, inspections, initial and final endorsement and permanent amortization.
