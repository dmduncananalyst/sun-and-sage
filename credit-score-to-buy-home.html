Credit Scores and Mortgage Qualification

There is no single credit score that works for every mortgage. The loan program, lender, and the rest of your financial profile affect the decision. Ask a lender to review your actual situation.

Understand the lender’s review

Credit is one part of the application alongside income, debts, assets, and the property. A consumer score you see in an app may differ from the score used for a mortgage.

Prepare before you apply

Review your credit reports for errors and keep payments current. Ask the lender before taking on new debt or making account changes during the mortgage process.

Before you apply

  • Review your reports for errors
  • Confirm the score and model the lender uses
  • Discuss income and debts together
  • Ask before opening new credit
  • Ask what to prepare before applying

California and San Fernando Valley context

For a San Fernando Valley purchase, the lender determines eligibility and loan terms, while the property can add costs such as taxes, HOA dues, insurance, repairs, and appraisal related issues. Compare written lender disclosures with the full cost of the specific home.

Understanding Credit in a Mortgage Review

A mortgage review considers more than a single score. The lender evaluates credit history, current obligations, documented income, available funds, and the requirements of the chosen program. A score shown by a consumer service may also differ from the score or assessment used in a lending decision.

Review your credit information for errors and discuss unresolved questions with the lender before changing accounts or borrowing again. Paying down a balance, closing an account, or taking on new debt can affect an application in different ways. An individual review is more useful than a universal score target presented as a guarantee of approval.

Understanding Written Loan Terms

Compare financing using the written terms for your application and property. The loan amount, repayment period, rate structure, mortgage insurance, lender charges, and cash required at closing work together. A change in one part of the offer can affect the others, so a quoted monthly principal and interest payment is only part of the comparison.

Ask which conditions remain before approval and what changes require a new review. New debt, a change in employment or documented income, or a property issue can affect financing before closing. Use the lender’s current disclosures and explanations rather than relying on a general program description as a promise of eligibility or final terms.
