Coordinating a Home Sale and Purchase

The transaction can often be structured so the sale and purchase are coordinated instead of leaving you with two long term housing payments. Options may include selling first, coordinating closing dates, negotiating a rent back, using a purchase or sale contingency, or discussing short term financing options with a lender.

Choose a timing structure early

Common approaches include selling first, coordinating closing dates, negotiating a rent back, using a purchase or sale contingency when available, or discussing short term financing options with a lender.

Every option has tradeoffs

A rent back depends on the other party agreeing to it. Contingencies can affect the strength or flexibility of an offer. Short term financing products depend on qualification and lender terms, so they should be discussed directly with a lender.

Coordinate everyone before the deadlines arrive

Dominique can help align the listing, replacement home search, lender, escrow, and closing timeline so the strategy is decided before you are under pressure from competing dates.

California and San Fernando Valley context

Selling in the San Fernando Valley is property specific. Pricing, preparation, replacement home inventory, financing, and the timing of competing listings can affect whether it makes sense to move now, wait, or coordinate a purchase with the sale.

Understanding the Overlap Between Two Homes

The sale of one home and purchase of another involve separate contracts and can move at different speeds. A planned closing date does not guarantee that the proceeds from one transaction will be available in time for the other. Review financing, contingency terms, and the practical possession dates together.

Temporary housing or an agreed period of continued occupancy can affect moving arrangements and expenses. Any occupancy after closing should be documented with the responsibilities and timing clearly explained. Compare a realistic delayed scenario as well as the preferred timeline so an unexpected change does not leave the household without funds or somewhere to stay.

Understanding a Home Sale Timeline

A sale includes preparation, exposure to buyers, negotiation, and the work required to complete an accepted contract. The time needed for each part depends on the property and the transaction. An attractive listing does not eliminate financing, inspection, title, or other issues that can affect closing.

When planning around another purchase or a move, distinguish an estimated schedule from a contractual deadline. Allow for uncertainty before committing to a date that depends on a buyer’s approval or an unfinished property issue. Current local information helps frame expectations, but it cannot promise a sale price or completion date.
